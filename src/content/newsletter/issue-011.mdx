## Fall Fundraiser 2026: Thank You for an Unforgettable Night

On October 1st we gathered for our largest fundraising effort to date, and what a night it was. Boston Women in Bioinformatics President [Yevgenia Khodor, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/yevgenia-khodor/) shared BWIB's plans for 2027, including conference sponsorships, cloud compute credits, back-to-work support, and research funding. Kelle Moley, M.D. moved us all with her experiences as a clinician and a clear reminder of how much unmet medical need remains.

Our brilliant panel of [Ashley Abel, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ashleyabel/), [Sheri Simmons, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sheri-simmons/), [Shruthi Mahalingaiah, M.D., M.S.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/shruthimahalingaiahmd/), and [Brandi Davis-Dusenbery, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/davis-dusenbery/) then took us through everything from menstrual cycle effects on gene expression to wearables, consumer health, and the institutional hurdles facing women's health. Missed it, or want to hear it again? Keep an eye out for a podcast of the panel later this month, recorded by Trevor Campbell.

A huge thank you to our sponsors and hosts: Diamond Age Data Science, Seqera, Zifo, ZS, Benchling, BioTeam, Sprout Informatics, and Brandeis University. And because we are 100% volunteer run, an enormous thank you to the organizing team: [Liyang Diao, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/liyang-diao-90933461/), [Samantha Klasfeld, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/samantha-klasfeld/), [Yevgenia Khodor, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/yevgenia-khodor/), [Emily Brodd](https://www.linkedin.com/in/emily-lachtara/), [Sakina Saif, M.S.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sakinasaif/), Iqra Amin, [Diveena Becker, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/diveena-becker-5945a771/), [Yaseswini Neelamraju, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/yaseswini-neelamraju-phd-14b7ba29/), and Nandeeni Suryawanshi, as well as our day-of volunteers Elyse Loveman, [Tracy A. Vu, M.S.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tracyavu), Anna McNiff, M.S., Ruoqiao Wang, and [Petra Palenikova, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/petra-palenikova-aa6330142/) We are so grateful to everyone who came out!

## Events Reflections

### Fall Fundraiser 2026: The Data, Tech, and Opportunities Driving a New Era of Women's Health

_[Lina Faller, Ph.D.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/linafaller/), VP of Boston Women in Bioinformatics_

For once, I wasn't running the event. At our Fall Fundraiser, I just got to be in the audience, and it reminded me why I keep showing up to BWIB.

Dr. Kelle Moley of Eli Lilly opened her keynote with the advice she got in med school: a woman is just a small man, so halve the dose. It was wrong, and it shaped decades of medicine. The panel that followed went deep on the data gap in women's health, and I walked out wanting a whole second event on what we, as the people who work with this data, can do about it. Maybe we'll host one.

And the hugs from familiar faces were the best part.

### Cambridge Science Carnival 2026

_[Tracy A. Vu, M.S.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tracyavu), Boston Women in Bioinformatics Communications Committee Member_

<figure>
  <img
    src="/photos/2026/ScienceFestival2026.png"

The Cambridge Science Carnival was held on Sunday, October 4th, a celebration of science and a day full of hands-on booths to inspire curiosity! Boston Women in Bioinformatics joined over 100 STEAM activities, hosting our own booth to engage with the community and highlight our organization's work.

Our booth was **DNA by Design**, where we made DNA and RNA bracelets based on sequences of different animals. Using different colored beads to represent each nucleotide, attendees could make sequences from animals like humans, cats, dogs, and mice. Some of our most creative visitors designed their own sequences, like chimeras and unicorns! Throughout the day, we shared how bioinformatics helps us understand and use DNA.

Thank you to all of our volunteers who staffed the booth and to everyone who stopped by!

## Don't Lose Us in Spam

To ensure you never miss an update, add **newsletter@boston-wib.org** and **communications@boston-wib.org** to your email contacts.

For guaranteed delivery, click below to see specific steps for your provider:

<details class="image-description">
<summary>Instructions</summary>

### Gmail (Desktop)

1. Click **Settings (Gear icon)** > **See all settings**.
2. Go to **Filters and Blocked Addresses** > **Create a new filter**.
3. In the **From** field, paste: `newsletter@boston-wib.org OR communications@boston-wib.org`
4. Click **Create filter**, check **Never send it to Spam**, and click **Create filter** again.

### Outlook (Web)

1. Click **Settings (Gear icon)** > **Mail** > **Junk email**.
2. Under **Safe senders and domains**, click **Add**.
3. Type `boston-wib.org` and press **Enter**.
4. Click **Save**.

### Apple Mail (iPhone/Mac)

1. Open the **Contacts** app and click **+** to create a new contact.
2. Name it **Boston WIB**.
3. Add both email addresses to the contact card and save.

</details>

_Tip: If an email is already in your Spam folder, open it and select **Not Spam** or drag it to your Primary inbox to train your provider._
