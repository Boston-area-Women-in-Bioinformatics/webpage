## Featured Speakers

### Xiaojing Sui, Ph.D.

[Dr. Xiaojing Sui](https://www.linkedin.com/in/xiaojing-sui-phd-18958870/) is an aging and proteomics researcher who earned her PhD at the University of Melbourne and conducted independent postdoctoral research at Northwestern University. She was recently recognized as a Rising Star in Proteomics and Metabolomics by the Journal of Proteome Research. Her work investigates how changes in protein structure contribute to aging, neurodegeneration, and loss of cellular resilience.

In her talk, she will present findings from quantitative structural proteomics studies in _C. elegans_, which show that nearly one-third of the proteome undergoes structural changes early in aging, well before physiological decline. Her research also demonstrates how localized protein stress can trigger organism-wide structural instability, which points to new opportunities to understand and potentially intervene in aging-related dysfunction.

### Aleena Iraqui

[Aleena Iraqui](https://www.linkedin.com/in/aleena-iraqui/) is an M.S. Bioinformatics student and Zanghi-Dow Fellow at Northeastern University whose research bridges computational biology, machine learning, and clinical data science. As a visiting graduate student at the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard, she applied causal inference and survival analysis to electronic health records to investigate the potential of diabetes medications in dementia prevention. Her work also spans drug discovery, neurobiology, and epilepsy classification, and she has led award-winning teams at several AI and biotechnology hackathons.

In her talk, she will explore how target trial emulation can help researchers draw more reliable causal conclusions from observational health data when randomized clinical trials are impractical or impossible. Drawing on her research comparing metformin and sulfonylureas in the NIH All of Us cohort, she will discuss the strengths and limitations of this approach and its role alongside AI and emerging methodologies in generating trustworthy medical evidence.

### Julia Gross, Ph.D.

[Dr. Julia Gross](https://www.linkedin.com/in/julia-gross) is the resident biologist at GXL, where she evaluates AI agents against real-world scientific research questions and helps translate researchers’ needs into more reliable agentic tools. Trained as an immunologist, she earned her Ph.D. through NIAID and Emory University and published first-author research in Nature Communications on antibiotic-induced inflammation mediated by TLR9. As part of the inaugural AI for Science Fellowship at Worldwide Studios, she developed Watch Agent Run, a tool for independently verifying what research agents actually retrieve and read. In her talk, she will present a prototype that systematically evaluates evidence across 44 groups of immunomodulatory drugs and 27 autoimmune conditions. Drawing on early findings, she will highlight common failure modes, such as mistaking animal studies for human evidence or confusing mechanistic explanations with clinical efficacy, and discuss how transparent, source-grounded evidence grading can improve the accuracy, reliability, and scientific usefulness of AI agents in biomedical research.
